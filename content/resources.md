# Resources

The texts, software, and references this book actually draws on, for readers who want to go deeper or set up their own environment.

## Primary texts

The notes and homework cite these throughout, particularly from Week 5 onward:

- Taylor, J. R. *Classical Mechanics* (University Science Books) — the main reference for equations of motion, conservation laws, and stability.
- Strogatz, S. H. *Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos* (CRC Press) — referenced in the Week 11 notes and Homework 6 for the calculus of variations and nonlinear/chaotic systems.

Neither is required to read this book, but both are good next steps if a topic needs more depth than the notebooks provide.

## Software

Every activity and homework notebook runs on the standard scientific Python stack:

- [Jupyter](https://jupyter.org/) — the notebook format the entire course is written in.
- [NumPy](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/) — array math and vectorized computation.
- [Matplotlib](https://matplotlib.org/stable/) — the plotting library used for every phase portrait, trajectory, and time series in the book.
- [SciPy](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/), especially [`scipy.integrate.solve_ivp`](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.integrate.solve_ivp.html) — the ODE solver introduced in Week 10 for chaotic systems and used in later homework.
- [MyST Markdown](https://mystmd.org/guide) — builds this site from the notebooks and Markdown pages. Useful if you're contributing content or want to build the site locally (see the [README](https://github.com/QuadriviumPress/modern-classical-mechanics#build)).

## Further reading

- [Wikipedia: Classical Mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_mechanics) and the topic-specific Wikipedia articles linked throughout the notes — used as quick background reading in nearly every week.
- [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/) — linked from a few homework sets (Weeks 4 and 6) for primary research on the systems being modeled.
